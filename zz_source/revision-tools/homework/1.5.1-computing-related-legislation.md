[2]** 1 mark: Computer Misuse Act 1990, **Offence 3** (unauthorised modification of computer material). 1 mark: deleting/altering the database is an unauthorised *modification*; the Act is about *authorisation* and the technician was no longer authorised to act on the system (and never authorised to destroy data) — it does not require a password to be stolen, only that the modification was unauthorised. *(Do not accept Offence 1 or 2.)*

**5. [4]** Levels-of-response **"discuss"** question — mark the **whole answer**, not point-by-point.

*Indicative content —*
- **FOR the powers:** serious crime and terrorism are increasingly planned using digital communications, so investigators need lawful interception; the power to demand encryption keys stops evidence being locked away; restricting *who may authorise* use provides accountability and ensures the powers are proportionate and used only for legitimate purposes.
- **AGAINST / concerns:** interception is a serious intrusion into privacy of people who may be innocent; risk of misuse or "mission creep" (powers used for minor matters); demanding keys can compromise everyone's data security; chilling effect on free communication.
- **Justified conclusion:** a clear, reasoned position, ideally conditional — e.g. *"the powers are acceptable only while authorisation remains restricted to senior officials, is limited to serious purposes and is subject to oversight."*

*Brief level descriptors:*
- **Level 1 (1):** one-sided or fragmented points; no conclusion.
- **Level 2 (2–3):** both sides touched on but limited development; weak conclusion.
- **Level 3 (4):** balanced discussion of investigative benefit **and** privacy cost, referring to the authorisation safeguard, with a justified conclusion.

**7. [2]** 1 mark: a stack is **LIFO** — the last item pushed on is the first popped off (removed from the top). 1 mark: a queue is **FIFO** — the first item enqueued is the first dequeued (removed from the front).
