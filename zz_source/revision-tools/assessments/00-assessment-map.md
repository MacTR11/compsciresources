## Diagnostic — Week 1

`01-diagnostic-week-1.md` in this folder. 45 minutes, /40, closed book, sat in the first week (the SOW's Week 1 homework note points students to it). It is a **GCSE-bridging** paper: Section A number bases and data (/12), Section B computational thinking and pseudocode reading (/14), Section C written computer systems basics (/14). Nothing beyond GCSE is assumed. Use it purely as a baseline: record the three section scores on each student's Personal Learning Checklist and use Section B to flag students needing early programming support. It is deliberately **not** a markbook column and never counts toward the working grade.

## Cumulative Assessed Pieces CAP1–CAP5

A CAP is a folder of evidence built over several weeks and handed in once; it assesses whether students **act on feedback** and keep earlier topics alive. CAP1–CAP4 are marked /20 with the common rubric below; CAP5 is the mock cycle and carries the mock mark /70.

| Rubric criterion (CAP1–CAP4) | Marks |
|------------------------------|-------|
| Completeness — every required item present and organised | 5 |
| Response to feedback — every dropped mark green-penned with a correct fix | 5 |
| Accuracy — corrected work is now right; knowledge shown to be secure | 5 |
| Practical evidence — code runs, is tested and annotated (or, for CAP2/CAP4, the error log and PLC are specific and up to date) | 5 |

**CAP1 (Weeks 5–7) — Topic 1.1 evidence folder.** Set in Week 5, due at the end of Week 7. Students submit their marked KA2 script with green-pen corrections, their full KA3 Mini-Paper 1.1 sitting, and their Programming Workbook 1 solutions from sessions 1–4. Marked /20 with the rubric; feedback returned Week 8 and entered in the CAP1 column.

**CAP2 (Weeks 9–13) — first cumulative folder (1.1–1.2 plus 2.1.1–2.1.3).** Set in Week 9, due at the end of Week 13. Students submit their Recap Checkpoint 1 script (sat timed in Week 10) with corrections, one 1.2.4 homework re-drafted to full marks after feedback, and their running error log. Marked /20 with the rubric; feedback in Week 14 alongside the EOY quiz.

**CAP3 (Weeks 18–20) — data structures in code.** Set in Week 18, due at the end of Week 20. Students submit working, tested Python implementations of a stack, a queue and a linked list from Programming Workbook 2 (binary search tree and hash table as stretch), each with annotated test evidence, plus their marked Mini-Paper 1.3 script (sat Week 19) with corrections. Marked /20 with the rubric.

**CAP4 (Weeks 24–25) — pre-Easter cumulative folder (1.1–1.4).** Set in Week 24, due at the end of Week 25. Students submit their timed Recap Checkpoint 3 script (1.1–1.4) with corrections, their Mini-Paper 1.4 sitting from Week 25, and a fully re-RAGged Personal Learning Checklist that names their two weakest rows going into Easter. Marked /20 with the rubric; feedback before the holiday so Easter revision is targeted.

**CAP5 (Weeks 27–29) — the mock cycle.** Students sit Mock Paper 1C in Week 27 under exam conditions; the paper mark /70 is the CAP5 markbook entry. Feedback is given in Week 28; by the end of Week 29 students submit full written re-attempts of their two weakest mock questions plus an updated PLC. The CAP5 mark is only confirmed in the markbook once this response is checked complete.

## EOY quiz — Week 14

The end-of-term synoptic check before Christmas is the **full Mini-Paper 1.2** (`mini-papers/1.2-software-and-software-development.md`), 35 minutes, /30, sat closed book — exactly the "Mini-Paper 1.2 timed" the SOW schedules for Week 14. Questions 6–8 (addressing modes, OOP, methodologies) pick up the 1.2.4 content held back from KA4. Coverage of 1.3 and 2.1.4 that same week comes from the scheduled class quizzes (1.3.4 and 2.1.4) and from Recap Checkpoint 2 over the holiday. There is no markbook column for the EOY quiz: record it as a percentage on the PLC and use it to set Christmas revision priorities.

## Mock — Week 27

`02-mock-paper-1c.md` in this folder. 90 minutes, /70, **unseen — do not release, set as homework, or file in the student-facing library**. It covers exactly what Year 12 has taught by Week 27: 1.1–1.4 complete, all of 2.1, and 2.2.1 (no 1.5, no 2.2.2, no 2.3). Mixed short-answer questions plus two 6-mark levels-of-response questions and one 9-mark starred levels-of-response question, with full mark scheme and banded criteria. Sat in Mock Week under exam conditions; the mark is entered as CAP5 (see above). Students prepare with Recap Checkpoint 6 (the Year 12 Pre-Mock Recap) in Week 26 — that paper stays formative.
